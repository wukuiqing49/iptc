Practical guide

How to add photo captions, credits and copyright on Android

Help the recipient understand what a photo shows, who created it and how it should be credited. Use each field for its purpose, then check the saved file.

Current version 1.4.0 · 2026-11-12

Choose the right fieldsWrite and save the informationWhy does another viewer show something different?Check before delivery

Choose the right fields

A headline briefly identifies the image. A caption explains the event, people and context. A byline identifies the creator, while a copyright notice states the rights information. Keywords help retrieval and do not replace a caption.

Example headline
Morning at the market
Example caption
A vendor arranges vegetables at the start of the day.
Byline
Your photography credit
Copyright notice
A rights statement you are entitled to use.

Write and save the information

  1. Select existing photos and open the editing workflow.
  2. Fill in a headline, caption, keywords, byline and copyright notice. Save repeated information in a preset if useful.
  3. Adjust individual captions where needed and generate the processed photos.
  4. Open a generated file and read the corresponding metadata fields back.

Why does another viewer show something different?

Viewers can read different IPTC or XMP fields, omit some fields or show cached information. Compare the same generated file and the same field before concluding that the write failed.

Check before delivery

  • Use accurate credits and rights statements, rather than copying demo text.
  • Verify names, places and facts in the caption.
  • Test the file format and viewing software used by the recipient.
